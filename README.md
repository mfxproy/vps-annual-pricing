## Why the CN2 GIA plans cost what they cost

CN2 GIA pricing looks absurd until you know what's behind it. China Telecom sells several tiers of IP transit into the mainland, and they're not interchangeable. AS4134 (the regular 163 backbone) is cheap and heavily congested — peak-hour packet loss can exceed 30%. AS4809 CN2 GT was supposed to fix that but has drifted back toward the same peak-hour behavior since 2019. CN2 GIA (AS4809) is the premium tier: stable, low packet loss, and expensive — wholesale transit can run as high as $120 per megabit, which is what drives the plan prices above. CTGNet (AS23764) is China Telecom's newer option that performs roughly on par with GIA at similar cost.

On the Los Angeles GIA-E plans, China-bound traffic is handed off across three carriers simultaneously: CN2 GIA (AS4809), CMIN2 (China Mobile, AS58807), and China Unicom Premium (AS10099). BandwagonHost runs 8 × 10 GbE CN2 GIA/CTGNet links across its two LA datacenters, and its flagship DC9 site carries the company's own claim of the best overall capacity and stability. Measured latency from mainland China to LA on these routes sits around 158–160 ms — close to the physical floor for a trans-Pacific hop, with the stability being the actual selling point.

The honest caveats: CN2 GIA capacity is limited, it tolerates DDoS poorly (BandwagonHost nullroutes attacked IPs), and it costs real money. If nobody in your audience connects from mainland China, the standard KVM plans do the same job for a third of the price. If they do, ordinary transit will visibly fall apart at 8 PM and no amount of RAM fixes that.
